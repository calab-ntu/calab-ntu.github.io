When massive stars collapse, they can trigger spectacular explosions known as core-collapse supernovae. Using 17 high-resolution, three-dimensional simulations with the GPU-accelerated GAMER code, we investigate how stellar rotation and magnetic fields influence this process. We find that even initially weak magnetic fields can grow to extreme strengths of over 10 quadrillion gauss near the newly forming neutron star. This remarkable amplification is driven by the interplay of rotation, turbulence, and the inward flow of stellar material. These powerful magnetic fields may influence how stars explode and leave distinctive signatures in gravitational waves, offering a new way to probe the extreme physics inside collapsing stars.
